How to Play Dogzzle

Everything you need to solve any Dogzzle puzzle: the four rules, how each tool works, a full step-by-step walkthrough of a real puzzle, and the strategies that crack even the biggest boards.

By Flying Tire Games · Updated November 20, 2026

The rules in 10 seconds

  1. One dog in every colored region.
  2. One dog in every row.
  3. One dog in every column.
  4. Dogs can't touch, not even corner to corner.

Every puzzle has exactly one answer, and you never need to guess.

The rules explained

Every Dogzzle board is a square grid split into colored regions. A 5x5 board has 5 regions and 5 hidden dogs, a 9x9 board has 9 regions and 9 dogs, and so on. Your job is to find exactly where every dog is hiding.

  • One dog per region. Each colored region holds exactly one dog, no matter how big or small the region is.
  • One dog per row and column. No two dogs can share a row or a column, so every row and every column ends up with exactly one dog.
  • No touching. Dogs can't be next to each other in any direction, including diagonally. Every dog has a one-square bubble of empty space around it.

Your tools

Pick a tool from the tray under the board, then tap a square.

🐶 Dog
Places a dog. Only use it when you're sure. If the dog is in the wrong spot, the square turns into a red X and the puzzle is over.
✕ X
Marks a square that can't hold a dog. Crossing out squares is how you narrow things down, so you'll use this tool the most.
? Maybe
Marks a square you think might hold a dog. It's a note to yourself and doesn't count as a guess.
⟲ Clear
Wipes all your X and ? marks so you can start your notes over.

Tip: A wrong dog ends the puzzle, but X and ? marks are free. Mark as much as you like, and only place a dog when the logic leaves no other choice.

Full walkthrough: solving a puzzle step by step

Let's solve a small 5x5 puzzle together. Rows are numbered 1 to 5 from top to bottom, and columns 1 to 5 from left to right. New marks in each step are outlined in white.

Step 1: Find a region stuck in one column

The purple region is three squares tall, and all three are in column 5. Column 5 needs exactly one dog, and the purple dog has to be somewhere in that column. That means no other region can use column 5, so put an X on the two blue squares at the bottom of column 5.

Step 2: Find a square that would block a whole region

Look at row 1, column 3 (the pink square next to the yellow region). If a dog sat there, it would share a row with the two yellow squares in row 1 and touch the yellow square in row 2 diagonally. The yellow region would have nowhere left for its dog, so that square can't hold a dog. X it.

Step 3: Do it again with the pink region

The pink region now has only two open squares, row 1 column 4 and row 2 column 4, and both are in column 4. Just like step 1, the pink dog owns column 4, so X the other squares in column 4 (rows 3, 4 and 5).

Step 4: Place your first dog

Count the open blue squares. After steps 1 and 3, only one is left: row 5, column 3. The blue region needs a dog, so that's it. Place the dog, then X everything it rules out: the rest of row 5, the rest of column 3, and every square touching it.

Step 5: Look for a row with one open square

Row 4 now has only one open square: column 1. Every row needs a dog, so place it there. That's the green dog, so X the rest of the green region, plus column 1 and the squares touching the new dog.

Step 6: Same trick, next row

Row 3 has just one open square left: column 5, in the purple region. Place the dog and X the squares around it.

Steps 7 and 8: Finish the board

The pink region has one open square left, row 1 column 4, so a dog goes there. That rules out row 1 column 2, which leaves the yellow region with one square: row 2, column 2. Place the last dog and the puzzle is solved!

Notice that we never guessed. Every dog was placed only after the other squares were ruled out. Bigger boards work the same way, they just take more steps.

Solving strategies

1. Start with the smallest regions

A region with two or three squares has very few places for its dog. Small regions, and regions squeezed into a corner, are usually the best place to start.

2. Regions stuck in one row or column

If every open square of a region is in the same row (or column), that region's dog owns that row. X every other square in it. This was the key move in steps 1 and 3 above.

3. Ask "would this square block a region?"

Pick a square and imagine a dog there. If that dog would cross out every open square of some other region, the square can't be right. X it. This is how we found step 2.

4. After every dog, clear its bubble

Each time you place a dog, X its whole row, its whole column, the rest of its region and the eight squares around it. Fresh X's almost always reveal a row, column or region with only one open square left.

5. Count regions against rows (advanced)

If two regions both fit entirely inside the same two rows, those two rows belong to them. Every other square in those two rows can get an X. The same works with three regions in three rows, and with columns. This trick is what breaks open the big 8x8 and 9x9 boards.

6. Use ? marks for pairs

When you know a dog is in one of two squares but not which, put a ? on both. Often a later step settles it, and the ? marks keep you from losing track.

Points and hints

Every puzzle you solve earns 25 points. If you're stuck, tap Hint to spend points, and the game marks a square that can't hold a dog. If you don't have enough points yet, the game lets you know. Try the strategies above first and save your points for the really tough boards.

The weekly challenge

Tap the target button on the main menu to play the weekly challenge. Each week features one breed: every dog in the puzzle is that breed, and you'll meet it with a breed intro card before you start.

  • The boards are big, so expect a real test.
  • No hints are allowed.
  • One wrong dog restarts the puzzle, so use X and ? marks and only place a dog when you're certain.
  • A new challenge arrives every week.

Achievements and the leaderboard

FAQ

Do you ever have to guess in Dogzzle?

No. Every Dogzzle puzzle has exactly one solution, and you can always reach it with logic. If you feel stuck, look for a region that only fits in one row or column, or a square that would block a whole region.

What happens if I place a dog in the wrong spot?

The square gets a red X and the puzzle ends. The game does not show you where the other dogs were. You can restart the puzzle or go back to the main menu.

How do hints work in Dogzzle?

You earn 25 points for every puzzle you solve. You can spend points on a hint, and a hint marks a square that can't hold a dog. Hints are not allowed in the weekly challenge.

How many levels does Dogzzle have?

Dogzzle has over 1,000 puzzles across Easy, Medium and Hard, with boards from small 5x5 grids up to big 9x9 grids, plus a new weekly challenge.

How many dog breeds are in Dogzzle?

There are 22 dog breeds, from the Cavalier King Charles Spaniel and Golden Retriever to the Corgi, Husky and Shiba Inu. You can see them all in the breeds menu.

Does Dogzzle have ads?

No. Dogzzle has no ads.
